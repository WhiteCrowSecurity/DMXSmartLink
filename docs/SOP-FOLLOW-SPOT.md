# Steer a light by hand (Follow Spot)

**Who this is for:** whoever is running lights during a service, a concert or a play, and needs to
keep a beam on someone who is moving.

**What you need:** the hub, and a phone or tablet on the same network. A game controller is optional.

---

## The one thing to know before you start

**The pad is a stick, not a map.** Push it and the light travels; let go and it stops where it is.
The light does not jump to wherever you touched.

That is how a real follow spot works, and it is the only way one person can follow another across a
room. If the beam moved to wherever your thumb landed, you would have to know in advance exactly
where on the pad "the third pew, house left" is — which is impossible in the moment that matters.

(If you *do* want to point at a known mark, that is **Aim** mode, further down.)

---

## Follow someone

1. On your phone, open the hub and tap **Follow Spot** in the nav bar.
2. Under **Fixture**, choose the light you want to steer. Moving heads are marked *(moving head)*.
3. Set **Speed**. Start at 35%.
4. **Push and hold** anywhere on the round pad, in the direction you want the beam to go. It keeps
   moving while you hold.
5. **Let go** and it stops.

On a phone, wherever your thumb first lands becomes the centre of the stick — so the control comes to
your thumb, not the other way round. Lift off and it re-centres.

### Getting the speed right

| It feels like | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| I keep overshooting the person | Turn **Speed** down |
| I cannot keep up with someone walking | Turn **Speed** up |
| I can follow them but cannot settle on their face | Turn on **Fine control** — quarter speed |

35% crosses the full range in about three seconds. A small room or a long throw usually wants less.

### If you lose the beam entirely

Press **Centre**. Pan and tilt go back to the middle of their travel, which is a known place to start
again from.

---

## Point at a known mark (Aim)

1. Tap **Aim** at the top of the page.
2. The pad becomes a map of everywhere the fixture can point.
3. Tap or drag, and the beam goes straight there.

Use this for the lectern, a door, a step — places that do not move. Tap **Track** to go back to the
stick.

---

## If the light moves the wrong way

Almost always this means the fixture is hung upside down.

1. With the fixture selected, turn on **Hung upside down** under **Rigging**.
2. Test it: push right, and the beam should now go right.

**Invert pan** and **Invert tilt** are separate switches for a fixture that is simply facing the
other way. Each one flips independently, so a hung fixture that is *also* inverted ends up back the
way it started — that is correct, not a bug.

These settings are **saved per fixture**. You set them once when the light is rigged, not before
every service. And because they are per fixture, you can steer a hung mover and a floor-standing one
together and both follow your thumb.

---

## Using a game controller

Plug an Xbox or PlayStation controller into the hub. It appears at the bottom of the Follow Spot page
within a few seconds — no setup, no pairing screen.

| Control | Does |
| --- | --- |
| Left stick | Pan and tilt |
| Right stick | Zoom and focus, on fixtures that have them |
| Triggers | Dimmer up / down |
| Left bumper (hold) | Fine control |
| Y | Centre |

It steers the fixture you selected on the page, exactly as the touch pad does.

---

## When you are finished

Press **Release**. The hub stops steering and lets the fixture go, so your scenes can drive it again.

**Release does not turn the light off.** It stays exactly as it is. If you want it dark, recall a
scene or take it down in Visual Control.

---

## Questions

**Can I use this on a par can or a wash, not a moving head?**
Yes. If a fixture cannot pan or tilt, the pad drives its **intensity on one axis and its colour on
the other** — handy for riding a house wash from the back of the room. The labels around the pad
change to say what it is actually driving, so you are never guessing.

**Will it disturb anything I have not touched?**
No. It only ever drives the two pad axes plus zoom, focus and the dimmer, and it does not write even
those until you move them. Selecting a fixture changes nothing at all — your scene stays exactly as
it was until you push the stick.

**What happens if my phone dies or the wifi drops mid-move?**
The light stops by itself within about half a second. It does not keep travelling.

**Can two people use it at once?**
One at a time per fixture. Selecting a fixture takes control from any Visual Control screen that is
open, so two operators are never fighting over the same light — that fight is what makes a mover
judder.

**Is it accurate enough for a tight beam at a long throw?**
On fixtures with 16-bit pan and tilt, yes — those get the full 65,536 steps of travel rather than
256, which is what stops small corrections arriving as visible steps. The page tells you when a
fixture is being driven that way.

**Does it work from outside the building?**
It is designed for the local network. Use it on the same wifi as the hub.
